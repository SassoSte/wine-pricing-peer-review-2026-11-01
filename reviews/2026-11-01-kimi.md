## Verdict

**Retain the lock as a provisional internal reference schedule — with narrowed reporting — and do not reopen the curve now.**

The mathematics is sound. The price/loss calculations and structural checks reproduce within
the tolerances below. Calibration statistics have the separate provenance listed below;
workbook recalculation was not independently reproduced. The implementation is internally
consistent by construction (positive, monotone, reference-independent, telescoping) and the
scope wrapper correctly rejects unsupported regions. The packet's self-disclosures are
unusually honest: every limitation I independently identified is acknowledged somewhere in the
packet, and the correction histories are preserved rather than rewritten.

What the layer does **not** have is independent market validation, and two pieces of its
evidence base are weaker than the headline numbers suggest: four of the ten calibration targets
are the model's own rounded frozen outputs, and the deep tail is pinned by treating a
post-disclosure price *ceiling* as an exact *endpoint*. Both are disclosed in the packet; the
practical consequence is narrower quoting, not refitting. No current evidence justifies a
refit; the right next step is pre-registered elicitation and real offer/sale outcomes, not more
post-disclosure acceptance rounds.

Recommended scope of use until further evidence arrives:

- **In scope:** internal anchor-relative reference for the four approved regions, seat-origin
  status, matched volume/mint/initial-release terms, quoted alongside the evidence tier of each
  price (overlapping categories defined in the Preserve/Stop list).
- **Out of scope:** Southwest and foreign regions (wrapper already enforces), regular-batch
  pricing via extrapolated discounts, resale/retail/delivered-cost claims, per-litre comparisons
  (volume unspecified), any presentation of fit MAE $5.97 as predictive accuracy, and any
  presentation of the three ceiling compliances as exact-price successes.

## Ranked findings

### Finding 1 — Calibration circularity: 4 of 10 "accepted prices" are the model's own outputs (evidence limit, high)

`calibrate_consistent_prices.py` line 29 sets, for the four `independent-checks-v2.json`
observations, `accepted_price = round(q['frozen_estimate'])` — the calibration target is the
previously disclosed model number, which the user then endorsed. Verified: all four targets
(790, 765, 690, 740) equal the rounded frozen estimates exactly. The remaining six targets
(`valuation-checks-v1.json`) are post-disclosure "Yes" acceptances of model-proposed prices
(1030, 940, 1090, 930, 840, 990).

Consequences:

- The headline fit MAE **$5.97** / max **$16.07** measures how well a 5-knot curve interpolates
  targets that were either proposed by the model or are the model's own numbers. It is not a
  predictive-accuracy estimate in any direction. The packet says "post-disclosure plausibility
  targets," but the stronger fact — that four targets *are* the model output rounded — is only
  discoverable by reading the calibration source. Stating it in the reference document is a
  **proposal** for the next version, not an edit this review makes.
- The genuinely informative calibration content is the four exact release anchors plus the
  sensible ordering of profile means. The leave-profile-out MAE **$39.73** and combined-profile
  holdout errors **$65–88** are the more honest internal accuracy scale — and they still score
  against post-disclosure acceptance targets, not independent prices.

This does not make the curve wrong — the anchors are real user-reported offer prices and the
shape is plausible — but "consistent arithmetic plus expert endorsement" is exactly what this
calibration establishes, and no more.

### Finding 2 — Constant regional ratios vs. the only direct evidence (structural assumption, high; direction corrected in round 2)

Shared `f` forces `P_regionA(x)/P_regionB(x) = anchor_A/anchor_B` for **every** profile
(NE/NW = 1.1 everywhere, modulo float noise). The only evidence bearing on regional shape —
the three pre-disclosure amber35/120 transfer judgments against the NE $450 calibration point —
differs from that baseline in all three cases, and the direction is **larger relative Northeast
premiums than the proportional baseline**, not compressed spreads (round-1 wording had this
backwards; corrected after the assessor's calculation, which I verified):

| Region | Expert ratio to NE | Model ratio to NE | Expert NE-minus-region gap | Model gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SE | 425/450 = 0.944 | 1075/1100 = 0.977 | $25 | $10.23 |
| Central | 400/450 = 0.889 | 1050/1100 = 0.955 | $50 | $20.45 |
| NW | 400/450 = 0.889 | 1000/1100 = 0.909 | $50 | $40.91 |

All three expert-implied ratios are **farther from 1** than the model's anchor ratios. This is
three approximate judgments at **one** profile, from a shared informant with earlier NE context
visible: it does **not** identify regional slopes, and a more flexible regional model fit to
current data would merely memorize acceptances. It is enough to keep the constant-ratio
assumption on the "untested, directionally questioned" list and to motivate Experiment 1 —
nothing more.

### Finding 3 — The deep tail is pinned by a ceiling treated as an exact endpoint (modeling choice, medium; sensitivity corrected in round 2)

The brown40/130 judgment was an **upper bound** ("can't imagine paying more than 300", recorded
*after* the three tail scenarios 120/271/357 were visible), clarified as "close to that… for
margin considerations" with no cost figure and **no numerical lower bound**. The refinement
sets the curve to hit 300 — the maximum value consistent with the ceiling — exactly
(299.99999999999994).

Consequences:

- All **37 profiles** in the loss segment (5.000200, 8.154152] are priced off a piecewise-linear
  segment ending at this ceiling-as-point.
- Sensitivity illustration (a **clearly labeled scenario, not an evidence interval**): the
  historical slower-tail scenario value 270.77 (rounded to 271) also satisfies the stated
  ceiling. Full-grid aggregate extrema are in `receipts-round2.json` (`R2_2`). The round-1
  sensitivity range was incorrect; one of its five examples was 3.41%. Round 1 also loosely
  called 271–300 an "admissible range"; corrected. **271 is not an elicited bound and this
  range must not be reported as an interval, band, or floor.**
- The packet discloses the ceiling-as-endpoint choice explicitly ("ceiling-active endpoint…
  not a verified economic floor"); this finding is about **downstream quoting**, not a new
  defect. The honest representation of a censored judgment in consumer outputs is a caveat,
  not a fabricated interval.

Separately, **63 of 125 profiles** (more than half the lattice) sit on the single
piecewise-linear segment between golden25/115 (loss 1.846228, the weakest calibration profile —
post-disclosure endorsements of ~790/765/690/740) and the $450 point at loss 5.000200. The
five middle-range point estimates (pre-disclosure, with earlier pricing examples visible)
diverged from model prices by MAE $105.05 / MAPE 14.68% before the post-disclosure endorsement;
the endorsement is evidence of plausibility, not of accuracy, and the packet says so.

### Finding 4 — Material attribute collapses inherited from ranking weights (identification limit, medium; causality wording corrected in round 2)

Using V28's nonnegative base coefficients as a money-loss coordinate imports ranking structure
into prices. Verified collapses:

- **Amber ≡ golden**: the amber→golden coefficient is exactly 0.0, so amber and golden have
  identical prices at every vintage/tare in every region. 125 profiles carry only **95 distinct
  prices** per region.
- **Pale/straw ≡ golden at 130 lb** (by design, preserving the elicited equivalence).
- **Golden→pale increment is tiny at the top**: about **$10.50** off $1100 at the (20,110)
  profile (0.9638%) — and cell-dependent under the piecewise-linear curve (2.3041% at (25,110);
  see `receipts-shade-premiums.json`).

The two largest middle-range misses (pale30/125: expert $800 vs model $521; amber30/115: expert
$700 vs model $563) are **not matched shade comparisons**, so they do not isolate shade as the
cause. The correct statement is a **hypothesis**: comparative-preference weights may understate
monetary shade premiums; the observed pattern is consistent with that hypothesis and with
several others. It is cheap to test (Experiment 3) and otherwise remains open.

### Finding 5 — One-ULP endpoint guard: harmless conservative rounding (documentation note, not a defect)

The reference states the final knot uses a one-ULP conservative adjustment "to avoid
floating-point overshoot above 300 USD." On this platform (macOS arm64, Python 3.12.16),
`1100·exp(−ln(1100/300))` evaluates to exactly `300.0` — no overshoot. That establishes nothing
about other platforms (libm `exp` may differ by an ULP), and the guard is harmless, defensible
cross-platform defense. No defect is claimed; the note exists only so future readers do not
mistake the guard for load-bearing numerics.

### Finding 6 — Unapproved-region prices remain visible in delivered artifacts (presentation risk, low)

The engine and the workbook (`consistent-prices-v2.xlsx`, all 1,250 prices matching runtime)
carry prices for Southwest and five foreign regions whose anchors are `model_inferred` — values
inherited from the v1 linear mapping that the packet itself documented as defective for grid
pricing. The approved wrapper correctly rejects these regions (verified in
`verify_pricing_lock.py`), and the config labels anchor status. The risk is purely that a
workbook consumer quotes an unapproved-region number. A scope watermark or separate sheet is a
**proposal** for a future version; not a lock issue.

### Finding 7 — Anchor confounding (already disclosed scope limit; no new defect claimed)

Region, seat-batch identity, commemorative design, and reported rarity (2–5% shares of
*different* regional populations) vary together across the four anchors; no calendar date or
absolute volume exists. The packet states each of these limits explicitly (`anchors-v1.json`
`analysis_limits`). I confirm only the arithmetic implication: nothing in the current data can
separate a provenance effect from a seat/rarity effect, so the four anchors measure a combined
premium. Any future "scarcity multiplier" derived from the 2/3/4/5% shares would be
fabricated — the packet already prohibits this; I endorse the prohibition.

## Proposed experiments (three, ranked; each bounded and falsifiable; protocols corrected in rounds 2–4)

All three are **proposals only, requiring preregistration**: no execution is authorized by this
review; profiles/cells, informants, decision rules, and stopping rules must be fixed in writing
before any elicitation or data collection begins; and any model change requires a versioned v3.
V28 remains locked regardless of outcomes.

### Experiment 1 — Regional-shape probe on the mid segment (tests finding 2)

*Proposal — requires preregistration and separate authorization before any elicitation or data collection.*


- **Incremental question:** prior rounds tested low-end transfer at the boundary (loss
  5.000200 and 8.154152) and middle-range levels in NE/NW only. The untested question is
  whether region/NE price ratios on the mid segment (1.846228, 5.000200) track the anchor
  ratios.
- **Hypothesis (direction corrected):** at matched mid-segment profiles, expert-implied
  region/NE ratios sit **farther from 1** than anchor ratios (larger relative NE premiums than
  the proportional baseline), consistent with the amber35/120 boundary pattern.
- **Profiles (losses verified on-segment in `R2_E1`):** amber30/120 (3.692435), plus two of
  {brown25/110 (2.846198), copper30/115 (3.384681), pale35/115 (4.384801), amber35/115
  (4.461707)}. Round 1's copper35/125 (5.769432) and golden35/125 (5.538693) lie **beyond** the
  5.000200 knot and were replaced.
- **Design:** 3 profiles × 4 regions = 12 pre-registered valuations, NE included as each
  ratio's denominator; one offering at a time; model prices frozen and hash-stamped before
  elicitation; raw answers verbatim with timestamps.
- **Units of evidence:** ratios are computed within informant and within profile
  (region price / NE price). 12 valuations yield 9 region/NE ratios, but with a single shared
  informant and a common NE denominator the ratios are not nine independent observations:
  **the independent observational unit is the informant** (round 4). Multiple profiles per
  informant are repeated within-unit measures — they screen for direction and consistency but
  do not multiply independent evidence. Two informants preferred; with one, any "detected"
  pattern routes to a second-informant confirmation round before motivating a v3 proposal.
  A fresh session reduces disclosure leakage but does **not** erase the informant's prior
  exposure to anchors and earlier rounds; treat answers as "pre-disclosure with prior
  context," not blind.
- **Decision rule (pre-registered):** ≥6 of 9 ratios deviating from anchor ratios by >5
  percentage points in the same direction → motivates a separately authorized v3 regional-shape
  experiment. ≥6 of 9 within ±5 pp → supports (does not prove) the constant-ratio assumption.
  Anything else → **inconclusive**: report and retain v2 without claiming equivalence; failing
  a rejection threshold does not close the question.
- **Stopping rule:** exactly one pre-registered round; no re-asking after disclosure.

### Experiment 2 — Prospective offer/sale outcome log

*Proposal — requires preregistration and separate authorization before any elicitation or data collection.*


- **Question:** how do locked v2 prices compare to real initial-release outcomes?
- **Records kept separate:** asks, acceptances, completed sales, and unsold/rejected offers.
  Only completed sales enter a transaction MAPE; asks are scored separately as ask-vs-model
  error. **Non-sales establish no price bound on their own** (round 4): an unsold listing can
  reflect exposure, timing, terms, or buyer composition as much as price, so it does not imply
  that the clearing price is below the ask. Record bids, asks, who rejected which offer,
  reasons and terms. Rejections do not automatically establish numerical willingness-to-pay
  bounds. Explicit statements of a maximum price are recorded as that party's stated limit,
  not a verified market bound. Keep these records separate from completed-sale error
  statistics. Non-sales are logged for coverage and context; they contribute no magnitude and
  no directional bound to any statistic unless the protocol pre-registers a controlled
  offering process (defined exposure window, documented bids and rejections).
- **Comparability and independence:** record region, batch status, volume, condition, terms,
  fees treatment, and dates; score only events matching the locked basis. Repeat stages of one
  offering form one event chain, not independent events. Coverage targets (regions × loss
  ranges) fixed in advance.
- **Scoring definitions (fixed in advance; rounds 3–4):** for completed sales, signed error =
  model price − realized price (USD); signed percentage error = (model − realized) /
  realized × 100; MAPE = mean absolute signed percentage error; signed bias = mean signed
  percentage error. For asks, the same formulas with the ask in place of the realized price,
  always reported separately from completed sales. Non-sales enter no error statistic and no
  sign-bias calculation (see records bullet: a passive non-sale establishes no bound, and
  rejections do not automatically establish numerical willingness-to-pay bounds; bid/rejection
  records stay separate from completed-sale error statistics). Every denominator n counts
  **independent events** (chains), not records: an offering that is listed, renegotiated, and
  eventually sold contributes n = 1. With small n, every observation is reported alongside any
  aggregate.
- **Forecasts frozen before any comparison; no interim refits.**
- **Decision rules (prospective, pre-registered — not guarantees):** e.g., completed-sale MAPE
  ≤ 20% with |signed bias| ≤ 10% supports continued provisional use with unchanged reporting;
  MAPE > 35% or |bias| > 20% motivates a versioned recalibration proposal. The pilot may end
  **inconclusive** on coverage or sample size; there is no automatic promotion to "validated"
  and no automatic closure at 8 events or 12 months (round-1 wording withdrawn). Assessment
  happens when the pre-registered coverage target is met or the observation window ends,
  whichever first.

### Experiment 3 — Shade-structure probes (tests finding 4; split into matched sub-tests)

*Proposal — requires preregistration and separate authorization before any elicitation or data collection.*


**Threshold logic (rounds 3–4).** The 3% separation threshold is a one-sided *detection*
threshold only. Failing to exceed it establishes **neither** equivalence **nor** confirmation
of the locked increments (0 for amber/golden; cell-dependent for pale/golden — 0.9638% at
(20,110), 2.3041% at (25,110), per `receipts-shade-premiums.json`): with one or two point
judgments per pair there is no variance estimate, so neither a rejection test nor an
equivalence test (e.g., TOST against a pre-registered ±3% margin) can actually be carried
out. Every "not detected" outcome below therefore retains v2 as a *working assumption* and
leaves the question open — it is never scored as confirming the model. A genuine equivalence
claim would need repeated independent judgments per pair supporting a pre-registered
equivalence-margin test; that is out of scope for this probe.

- **3a — amber vs golden (matched):** 4 pre-registered matched cells — (30,115), (30,120),
  (35,110), (25,125) — same-cell pair valuations, model predictions frozen and hashed first.
  The locked premium is exactly 0 in all four cells (verified,
  `receipts-shade-premiums.json`), so the baseline is uniform. Per pair, record the sign:
  `+` if the judged amber premium over golden exceeds +3%, `−` if it is below −3% (golden
  judged above amber by >3%), `0` otherwise. Let n₊, n₋, n₀ be the counts. The outcomes are a
  **mutually exclusive partition, evaluated in this order** (round 4 — the round-3 rules
  overlapped: 3 positive + 1 negative satisfied both "detected" and "mixed sign"):
  1. **Mixed sign:** n₊ ≥ 1 **and** n₋ ≥ 1 (any magnitudes) → judgments are
     context-dependent or noisy; record cell positions, retain v2, no refit on four pairs.
  2. **Detected:** n₊ ≥ 3 (direction: amber above golden) or n₋ ≥ 3 (direction: golden above
     amber) → motivates a separately authorized v3 shade-term experiment, subject to the
     informant rule below; the sign is recorded either way.
  3. **Inconclusive:** n₊ = 2 or n₋ = 2 (with the other count 0) → retain v2; report.
  4. **Not detected:** n₊ + n₋ ≤ 1 → separation not detected at this power; equivalence is
     **not** established (see threshold logic); the question stays open.
  (Round 1's "four-of-five" criterion against two cells was internally mismatched; fixed in
  round 2.)
- **3b — pale vs golden:** 2 pre-registered pairs, **both at 110 lb**: (20,110) and (25,110).
  Tare is held constant within this sub-test so the pale premium is not confounded with the
  tare gradient (0.5385 loss per 5 lb) or with the designed golden/pale equivalence at
  130 lb — which is elicited structure, out of scope here. (Round 2 labeled the sub-test
  "at 110 lb" while listing (20,110) and (20,115) — a contradiction; fixed in round 3. If
  tare-dependence of the pale premium is itself the question, that is a *separate* probe
  requiring its own pre-registered cells, e.g., (20,125) vs golden (20,125); it is not
  smuggled into this one.)
  **Cell-specific baselines (round 4):** because f is piecewise linear, the locked
  pale-over-golden premium is **not** a universal ~1% — it is 0.9638% at (20,110) and
  **2.3041%** at (25,110) (verified, `receipts-shade-premiums.json`; e.g., 1.3397% at
  (30,110)). Round 3's "~1%" hypothesis used the (20,110) figure for both cells and was wrong
  for (25,110). Each pair is therefore scored against its own baseline: for pair *i* with
  locked baseline bᵢ, let dᵢ = judged premium − bᵢ, signed `+` if dᵢ > +3 percentage points,
  `−` if dᵢ < −3 pp, `0` otherwise. Outcomes (a partition):
  1. **Mixed sign:** one pair `+` and one `−` → inconclusive-by-conflict; record vintage
     positions, retain v2, no refit.
  2. **Detected:** both pairs `+` (premiums exceed cell baselines by >3 pp) or both `−`
     (below baselines by >3 pp) → motivates v3 examination of the increment, subject to the
     informant rule below; sign recorded.
  3. **Inconclusive:** exactly one pair non-zero.
  4. **Not detected:** both pairs `0` → deviation not detected at this power; the locked
     cell-specific premiums are **not** thereby confirmed; the question stays open.
- **Shared-informant dependence (rounds 3–4):** with a single shared informant, all pairs in
  a sub-test are correlated judgments. **The independent observational unit is the informant**
  (round 4 — round 3's "informant × cell" still implied within-informant independence and is
  corrected): multiple cells per informant are repeated within-unit measures that screen for
  direction and consistency but contribute one independent unit in total. One informant's
  systematic belief moves every pair in the same direction, so a "consistent sign" result
  from one informant is evidence of that informant's belief as much as of any market fact.
  Protocol: ≥2 informants pre-registered where possible. If only one informant is available,
  a "detected" outcome does **not** route to a v3 proposal directly; it routes to a single
  confirmation round with a second informant (same frozen predictions, same cells), and only
  consistent replication across informants — two independent units — motivates the v3
  proposal. A fresh session mitigates disclosure leakage but does not erase prior exposure.
- **Stopping rule:** one round per sub-test, plus at most the single second-informant
  confirmation round where triggered. Positive evidence motivates a separately authorized v3
  experiment only — it does not automatically warrant a shade term, and nothing here touches
  V28.

If only one experiment is run, run Experiment 1: it attacks the strongest unexamined structural
assumption with the cheapest design.

## Round-3 amendments (protocol hardening, self-initiated)

Two residual weaknesses in the experiment protocols were flagged for repair; both are fixed in
this revision, changing **protocol text only** — no finding, verdict, or numeric receipt is
affected, and no packet file is touched.

1. **Shade decisions (E3).** The round-2 rules treated failure to exceed the 3% threshold as
   supporting the locked 0 / ~1% increments. That is the same fallacy the assessment corrected
   in E1: a one-sided detection threshold cannot establish equivalence, and with one or two
   point judgments per pair there is no variance estimate to run either a rejection or an
   equivalence (TOST-style) test. E3 now carries explicit threshold logic: every
   "not detected" outcome retains v2 as a working assumption and leaves the question open —
   never as confirmation of the model — and a genuine equivalence claim is declared out of
   scope for this probe. **Mixed-sign results are now an explicit outcome** in both sub-tests
   (≥1 pair >3% in each direction in 3a; opposite-direction pairs in 3b), routed to
   "context-dependent or noisy judgments; retain v2; no refit," distinct from both detection
   and non-detection. The exactly-2-of-4 consistent-sign case in 3a is labeled inconclusive.
2. **Protocol details (E2/E3).** The 3b design contradicted itself on tare — labeled
   "at 110 lb" while listing (20,110) and (20,115). Fixed: both pairs are now at 110 lb —
   (20,110) and (25,110) — holding tare constant so the pale premium is not confounded with
   the tare gradient (0.5385 loss per 5 lb) or the designed golden/pale equivalence at
   130 lb; tare-dependence, if wanted, is a separate pre-registered probe and is not smuggled
   in. **Shared-informant dependence** is now treated structurally: the unit of independent
   evidence is informant × cell, one informant's consistent-sign result is evidence of that
   informant's belief as much as of any market fact, and a single-informant "detected" outcome
   routes to a second-informant confirmation round rather than directly to a v3 proposal.
   **E2 scoring denominators** are now fixed in advance: signed error = model − realized (USD),
   signed percentage error = (model − realized)/realized × 100 against completed sales (asks
   scored identically but reported separately), non-sales contributing only a censoring
   indicator, and every n counting independent event chains rather than records, with all
   observations reported alongside aggregates at small n.

## Round-4 amendments (assessment follow-up: three remaining items resolved)

The round-3 assessment accepted the detection/equivalence split, the tare fix, and the error
formulas, and flagged three substantive remainders. All three are resolved here; protocol text
only — no finding, verdict, or packet file is affected.

1. **Non-sales imply no clearing-price bound.** Round 3 described unsold offers as censored
   signals implying "clearing price below ask." A passive non-sale establishes no such bound —
   absence of a sale can reflect exposure, timing, terms, or buyer composition. E2 and Q9 now
   state: non-sales are logged for coverage and context and enter no magnitude statistic and no
   sign-bias calculation. Record bids, asks, who rejected which offer, reasons and terms.
   Rejections do not automatically establish numerical willingness-to-pay bounds. Explicit
   statements of a maximum price are recorded as that party's stated limit, not a verified
   market bound. These records are kept separate from completed-sale error statistics. (This
   supersedes the round-3 appendix's "censoring indicator" language and the corresponding
   PR-description bullet.)
2. **Cell-specific shade baselines.** Round 3's 3b hypothesis ("exceeds the locked ~1%") used
   the (20,110) premium for both cells. The locked pale-over-golden premium is cell-dependent
   under the piecewise-linear curve: 0.9638% at (20,110) and **2.3041% at (25,110)** (also
   1.0789% at (20,115), 1.3397% at (30,110); amber/golden exactly 0 in all four 3a cells) —
   computed from `consistent-price-config-v2.json` and recorded in
   `receipts-shade-premiums.json`. 3b now scores each pair against its own baseline:
   dᵢ = judged premium − bᵢ, flagged only when |dᵢ| > 3 percentage points.
3. **Decision-rule partition and independence unit.** Round 3's 3a rules overlapped (3
   positive + 1 negative satisfied both "detected" and "mixed sign"). 3a and 3b now evaluate
   sign counts (n₊, n₋, n₀) as an ordered, mutually exclusive partition — mixed-sign checked
   first, then detected, then inconclusive, then not detected — every sign-count combination
   lands in exactly one outcome. Detection thresholds: 3a: ≥3 of 4 with mixed-sign precedence;
   3b: 2 of 2 with mixed-sign precedence. The independence unit is corrected from
   "informant × cell" (which still implied within-informant independence) to **the informant**:
   multiple cells per informant are repeated within-unit measures, and a single-informant
   "detected" routes to a second-informant confirmation round. The same correction is applied
   to E1's units-of-evidence bullet ("informant × profile" → informant).
