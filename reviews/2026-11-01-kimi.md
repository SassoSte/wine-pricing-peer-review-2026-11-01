## Verdict

**Retain the lock as a provisional internal reference schedule — with narrowed use — and do not reopen the curve now.**

The mathematics is sound and everything the packet claims about itself reproduces exactly under
independent recomputation. The implementation is internally consistent by construction (positive,
monotone, reference-independent, telescoping) and the scope wrapper correctly rejects unsupported
regions. The packet's self-disclosures are unusually honest: every limitation I independently
identified is acknowledged somewhere in the packet, and the correction histories are preserved
rather than rewritten.

What the layer does **not** have is independent market validation, and two pieces of its evidence
base are weaker than the headline numbers suggest: four of the ten calibration targets are the
model's own rounded frozen outputs, and the deep tail is pinned by treating a post-disclosure
price *ceiling* as an exact *endpoint*. Neither is hidden — but both should narrow how the
numbers are quoted. No current evidence justifies a refit; the right next step is blind,
pre-registered elicitation and real offer/sale outcomes, not more post-disclosure acceptance
rounds.

Recommended scope of use until further evidence arrives:

- **In scope:** internal anchor-relative reference for the four approved regions, seat-origin
  status, matched volume/mint/initial-release terms, quoted alongside the evidence tier of each
  price (see Preserve/Stop list).
- **Out of scope:** Southwest and foreign regions (wrapper already enforces), regular-batch
  pricing via extrapolated discounts, resale/retail/delivered-cost claims, per-litre comparisons
  (volume unspecified), any presentation of fit MAE $5.97 as predictive accuracy, and any
  presentation of the three ceiling compliances as exact-price successes.

## Ranked findings

### Finding 1 — Calibration circularity: 4 of 10 "accepted prices" are the model's own outputs (evidence limit, high)

`calibrate_consistent_prices.py` line 29 sets, for the four `independent-checks-v2.json`
observations, `accepted_price = round(q['frozen_estimate'])` — i.e., the calibration target is
the previously disclosed model number, which the user then endorsed. Verified in
`G2_self_referential_targets`: all four targets (790, 765, 690, 740) equal the rounded frozen
estimates exactly. The remaining six targets (`valuation-checks-v1.json`) are also
post-disclosure "Yes" acceptances of model-proposed prices (1030, 940, 1090, 930, 840, 990).

Consequences:

- The headline fit MAE **$5.97** / max **$16.07** measures how well a 5-knot curve interpolates
  targets that were either proposed by the model or are the model's own numbers. It is not a
  predictive-accuracy estimate in any direction. The packet does say "post-disclosure
  plausibility targets" — but the stronger fact, that four targets *are* the model output
  rounded, is only discoverable by reading the calibration source. It should be stated in
  `domestic-release-reference-v2.md`.
- The genuinely informative calibration content is: (a) the four exact release anchors, and
  (b) the profile *means* being ordered sensibly. The leave-profile-out MAE $39.73 and
  combined-profile holdout errors $65–88 are the honest internal accuracy scale, and even they
  score against acceptance data.

This does not make the curve wrong — the anchors are real user-reported offer prices and the
shape is plausible — but "consistent arithmetic plus expert endorsement" is exactly what this
calibration establishes, and no more.

### Finding 2 — Constant regional ratios are contradicted (weakly, systematically) by the only direct evidence (structural assumption, high)

Shared `f` forces `P_regionA(x)/P_regionB(x) = anchor_A/anchor_B` for **every** profile
(verified: NE/NW = 1.1 everywhere, `H_`). The only evidence that bears on regional shape — the
three blind-ish amber35/120 transfer judgments against the NE $450 calibration point — points
the other way in all three cases (`G_transfer_expert_implied_regional_ratios_vs_model`):

| Ratio | Expert-implied | Model-implied |
|---|---:|---:|
| SE/NE | 425/450 = 0.944 | 1075/1100 = 0.977 |
| Central/NE | 400/450 = 0.889 | 1050/1100 = 0.955 |
| NW/NE | 400/450 = 0.889 | 1000/1100 = 0.909 |

n = 3 approximate judgments, elicited with earlier NE numbers visible — far from decisive, and
the packet flags regional transfer as an assumption. But the direction is unanimous: regional
price spreads may **compress** at the low end rather than stay proportional. A more flexible
regional-shape model is *not* identifiable from current data (it would memorize three
acceptances); the correct response is Experiment 1, not a refit.

### Finding 3 — The deep tail is pinned by a ceiling treated as an exact endpoint (modeling choice, medium)

The brown40/130 judgment was an **upper bound** ("can't imagine paying more than 300", recorded
*after* the three tail scenarios 120/271/357 were visible), clarified as "close to that… for
margin considerations" with no cost figure. The refinement sets the curve to hit 300 —
the **maximum** admissible value — exactly (`C_`: locked price 299.99999999999994).

Consequences:

- All **37 profiles** in the loss segment (5.000, 8.154] are priced off a straight line to this
  ceiling-as-point (`K_deep_tail_segment_span`).
- The choice is systematically **high** within the admissible set: the slower tail (271) also
  satisfies the stated ceiling and would lower deep-tail prices by 5.8–9.7%
  (`J_tail_sensitivity_endpoint_271_vs_300`: e.g., NE copper40/130 $354.92 → $334.42).
- The packet discloses all of this ("ceiling-active endpoint… not a verified economic floor"),
  which is correct handling. The residual issue is downstream: any output that quotes a single
  deep-tail number without the caveat overstates precision. The honest representation of a
  censored judgment is an interval, not a point.

Additionally, **63 of 125 profiles** (more than half the lattice) sit on the single
interpolation segment between golden25/115 (loss 1.846, the weakest calibration profile —
post-disclosure endorsements of ~790/765/690/740) and the anchored $450 at loss 5.000
(`K_mid_segment_span`). The entire mid-range is one straight line in (L, f). The six
middle-range blind estimates diverged from that line by MAE $105 / 14.7% before the
post-disclosure endorsement; the endorsement is evidence of plausibility, not of accuracy, and
the packet says so.

### Finding 4 — Material attribute collapses inherited from ranking weights (identification limit, medium)

Using V28's nonnegative base coefficients as a money-loss coordinate imports ranking structure
into prices. Verified collapses (`E_`):

- **Amber ≡ golden**: the amber→golden coefficient is exactly 0.0, so amber and golden have
  identical prices at every vintage/tare in every region. 125 profiles carry only **95 distinct
  prices** per region.
- **Pale/straw ≡ golden at 130 lb** (by design, preserving the elicited equivalence).
- **Golden→pale increment is tiny**: about **$10.50** off $1100 at the top profile (~1%).

These may be true in the elicited domain. But note where the largest blind middle-range
discrepancies landed: pale30/125 (expert $800 vs model $521, −35%) and amber30/115 (expert $700
vs $563, −20%) — precisely the profiles where shade premiums do the work. One expert, six
checks, post-disclosure reversal; not conclusive. It is, however, exactly the pattern you would
expect if comparative-preference weights understate monetary shade premiums, and it is cheap to
test (Experiment 3).

### Finding 5 — The one-ULP endpoint rationale does not reproduce as stated (documentation, trivial)

The reference states the final knot uses a one-ULP conservative adjustment "to avoid
floating-point overshoot above 300 USD." On this platform (macOS arm64, Python 3.12.16),
`1100·exp(−ln(1100/300))` evaluates to exactly `300.0` — no overshoot
(`C_overshoot_without_ulp = false`). The guard is harmless and defensible as cross-platform
defense (libm `exp` may differ by an ULP elsewhere), but the stated mechanism did not reproduce.
Not a defect; a footnote-level correction.

### Finding 6 — Unapproved-region prices remain visible in delivered artifacts (presentation risk, low)

The engine and the workbook (`consistent-prices-v2.xlsx`, all 1,250 prices matching runtime)
carry prices for Southwest and five foreign regions whose anchors are `model_inferred` — values
inherited from the v1 linear mapping that the packet itself documented as defective for grid
pricing (section 3 of the prompt). The approved wrapper correctly rejects these regions
(verified in `verify_pricing_lock.py`), and the config labels anchor status. The risk is purely
that a workbook consumer quotes, e.g., a Western Europe or Southwest number. A scope watermark
or separate sheet for unapproved regions would close it; not a lock issue.

### Finding 7 — Anchor confounding is real and correctly disclosed (scope limit, acknowledged)

Region, seat-batch identity, commemorative design, and reported rarity (2–5% shares of
*different* regional populations) vary together across the four anchors; no calendar date or
absolute volume exists. The packet states each of these limits explicitly (`anchors-v1.json`
`analysis_limits`). I confirm the arithmetic implication: nothing in the current data can
separate a provenance effect from a seat/rarity effect, so the four anchors measure a combined
premium. Any future "scarcity multiplier" derived from the 2/3/4/5% shares would be fabricated —
the packet already prohibits this; I endorse the prohibition.

## Answers to the reviewer's ten questions (condensed)

1. **Units/encoding/signs/interpolation/boundary/reference translation:** correct; verified
   independently (items 1–9 above). The −1e-10 clamp and the domain-membership validation in
   `attribute_loss` are appropriate; extrapolation beyond the final knot exists in code but is
   unreachable on the lattice.
2. **V28 weights as a 1-D loss coordinate:** defensible as the minimal consistent monetary
   completion of an already-elicited ranking, and far better than the failed pair-score
   conversion. The cost is real collapses (finding 4) and the assumption that preference-score
   units map linearly to log-price; current evidence neither confirms nor refutes it.
3. **Shared f across regions:** it imposes exact constant ratios; that is stronger than the
   evidence (finding 2). A more flexible model is not identifiable now — it would memorize
   acceptances. Correct posture: retain + Experiment 1.
4. **Profile averaging / equal weights / monotonic pooling:** reasonable and conservative; with
   4 profiles × 10 acceptances, equal profile weighting is the right choice. What is identified:
   four interior knot heights. Everything else is functional form.
5. **Near-but-not-above-300 as active endpoint:** it overstates precision in the deep tail
   (finding 3). Correct representation: report the segment as an interval anchored by the
   admissible tail family (≈271–300 at the corner) until transaction data selects within it.
6. **Are positivity/monotonicity/reference-independence sufficient:** for an internal reference,
   yes, provided every quoted price carries its evidence tier. Additional cheap checks worth
   adding to the test suite: (a) distinct-price count per region (95) as a regression tripwire;
   (b) a tripwire that any new anchor outside ±20% of the current model price forces
   versioning, per the packet's own change policy.
7. **Legitimate to retain as provisional guide:** yes. First-estimate discrepancies (MAE $105)
   plus a genuine post-disclosure endorsement support "operationally useful plausibility
   reference"; they do not support "validated." The packet's own wording already sits at this
   level.
8. **Confounding:** yes, materially limiting for transfer outside the four anchor regions and
   for any rarity decomposition (finding 7).
9. **Are real offer/acceptance/sale outcomes needed next:** yes — they are the only evidence
   class that can promote the layer past "provisional" (Experiment 2). Include offers that did
   not sell, with date, terms, volume, condition, and batch status.
10. **Is the lock appropriate:** yes. Narrow the *reporting*, not the mathematics (Preserve/Stop
    list). Do not start an open-ended refinement cycle.

## Proposed experiments (three, ranked; each bounded and falsifiable)

### Experiment 1 — Blind regional-shape probe (tests finding 2; highest information per dollar)

- **Hypothesis:** regional price ratios compress below anchor ratios as attribute loss grows
  (expert-implied low-end ratios were unanimously flatter than model-implied).
- **Minimal math change:** none to the lock. Collect 6 blind point-or-range valuations at
  pre-registered mid-loss profiles (suggest: amber30/120, copper35/125, golden35/125 — losses
  ≈2.5–5.0, on the long interpolation segment) in ≥3 regions, randomized order, one offering at
  a time, no model numbers or earlier answers visible.
- **Baseline:** current v2 prices frozen and hash-stamped before elicitation (the packet's
  existing freeze discipline).
- **Leakage controls:** fresh session; no disclosure of anchors, model prices, or prior rounds;
  record raw answers verbatim with timestamps; one informant minimum, two preferred.
- **Acceptance/failure:** if ≥4 of 6 blind ratios deviate from anchor ratios by >5 percentage
  points in the same direction, a v3 with a regional slope term is justified. Otherwise the
  constant-ratio assumption gains its first real support and the question closes.
- **Stopping rule:** exactly one pre-registered round; no re-asking after disclosure; whatever
  the outcome, the result is appended as evidence, and the v2 lock is only superseded by a
  versioned v3.

### Experiment 2 — Prospective offer/sale outcome log (tests the only thing that can validate pricing)

- **Hypothesis:** locked v2 prices track actual initial-release outcomes within ±20% MAPE with
  no systematic sign bias.
- **Minimal math change:** none. Log the next 5–10 real events — offers, acceptances, completed
  sales, **and** offers that did not sell — with date, region, batch status, volume, condition,
  terms, and fees treatment. Score against v2 prices frozen at log start.
- **Leakage controls:** events recorded before any model comparison is shown to the seller-side
  informant; completed sales and asking prices scored separately.
- **Acceptance/failure:** MAPE ≤ 20% and signed error within ±10% → promote the layer from
  "provisional" to "validated for initial-release offer guidance." MAPE > 35% or |sign bias| >
  20% → reopen calibration with real data (new version). Between: retain with wider stated band.
- **Stopping rule:** close after 8 events or 12 months, whichever first; no interim refits.

### Experiment 3 — Shade-collapse probe (tests finding 4)

- **Hypothesis:** amber and golden are not monetarily equivalent at matched vintage/tare, and/or
  the golden→pale premium exceeds ~1% near the top of the lattice.
- **Minimal math change:** none. 4–6 blind paired valuations: amber vs golden at two matched
  (vintage, tare) cells; pale vs golden at 110 lb and at 130 lb. Model predictions frozen and
  hashed first.
- **Leakage controls:** pairs presented without model numbers; order randomized; absolute-price
  framing ("what would you expect each to fetch at initial release, same terms").
- **Acceptance/failure:** if ≥4 of 5 blind comparisons separate amber from golden by >3%, the
  shared-L coordinate is misspecified in shade and a v3 shade-specific discount term is
  warranted. Confirmed equivalence instead converts finding 4 from open question to supported
  structure.
- **Stopping rule:** one round; the result feeds only a versioned v3, never an in-place edit.

If only one experiment is run, run Experiment 1: it attacks the strongest unexamined structural
assumption with the cheapest design.

## Preserve / Stop list

**Preserve (do not touch):**

- **V28 comparative lock** — independently verified (46 hashes); ranking model untouched by
  this review; good ranking was never evidence of good prices and this review changes nothing
  about it.
- **The v2 monetary curve, anchors, and knot structure** — as a provisional reference.
  Internally consistent, honestly documented, exactly reproducible.
- **The evidence-handling discipline** — frozen-prediction hashing, preserved correction
  histories, separation of initial estimates from post-disclosure endorsements, explicit
  "ceiling compliance ≠ point accuracy" framing. This is exemplary and should be the template
  for future rounds.
- **The scope wrapper and change policy** — region rejection works; versioning requirement is
  correct.

**Narrow (reporting, not mathematics):**

- Quote every price with its evidence tier: anchor-exact (4) / in-span interpolated (230) /
  NE-calibrated tail / regional-transfer-assumed (918 profiles flagged in code already —
  surface the flag in the workbook and any consumer output).
- Do not quote single numbers for loss > 5.000 (37 deep-tail profiles) without the
  ceiling-endpoint caveat and the ≈271–300 admissible endpoint range.
- State in `domestic-release-reference-v2.md` that four calibration targets equal rounded model
  outputs (currently visible only in calibration source).

**Stop:**

- Stop citing fit MAE $5.97 / max $16.07 as accuracy evidence in any summary; use the
  leave-profile-out $39.73 and middle-range $105.05 figures as the honest internal scale.
- Stop counting the three "≤ $300" compliances as successes; one ceiling compliance is
  one bit of information, not a confirmation.
- Stop any use of the workbook's Southwest/foreign prices; they derive from a mapping the
  packet itself retired.
- Do not derive scarcity multipliers from the 2/3/4/5% batch shares (packet already bans this;
  endorsed).
